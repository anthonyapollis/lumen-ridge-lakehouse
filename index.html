Scope

A rehearsal, not a production claim. The evidence comes from a synthetic test. Measured results, list-price workload and Azure charges are labelled separately throughout.

The assignment

Find the failures while failure is still cheap.

The brief was blunt: seven sources, finance-grade reconciliation, and a USD 200 monthly ceiling with a kill switch at USD 190. Nobody wanted to discover on live data that a pipeline double-counted a bet, that a report depended on raw fact-to-fact joins, or that idle networking cost more than useful compute.

So the team built a dress rehearsal. Two generators produced a dirty month of online and retail activity, including late arrivals, re-sent files, conflicting versions and missing settlements. The platform around it was real: Azure Databricks, Unity Catalog, Terraform, dbt, Power BI, ClickHouse and GitHub.

The result is a practical account of the controls that mattered: immutable Bronze evidence, explicit Silver rules, dimensional Gold models, reconciliation, cost boundaries and a promotion barrier between development and production.

Evidence and limits

Clear about what was measured.

The row counts, duplicate-key checks and 53-second summary build are measured rehearsal results. The duplicate result covers 25 tested Silver and Gold grains; it is not a claim that every future source is duplicate-free. Azure charges and Databricks list-price usage are different measures and are shown separately.
